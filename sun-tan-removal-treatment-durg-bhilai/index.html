Sun tanning occurs when the skin produces additional melanin in response to ultraviolet exposure. It may cause the face, neck, arms, hands or feet to appear darker or uneven.

Frequent outdoor exposure and Chhattisgarh’s strong sunlight can contribute to tanning, pigmentation, dryness and premature ageing.

Common Signs of Sun-Damaged Skin

  • Uneven skin tone
  • Darkened forehead or cheeks
  • Tanned arms, hands or feet
  • Dull and rough skin
  • Visible sunspots
  • Patchy pigmentation
  • Fine lines
  • Loss of natural brightness

Dermatologist Evaluation

Not every dark patch is caused by tanning. Melasma, post-inflammatory pigmentation, friction-related pigmentation and certain medical conditions can resemble a tan.

A skin assessment helps identify the concern before beginning treatment.

Treatment Approach

Depending on skin type and severity, care may include:

  • Appropriate home skincare
  • Broad-spectrum sunscreen
  • Chemical peels
  • Exfoliation-based procedures
  • Hydrating treatments
  • Pigmentation-focused treatment
  • Selected laser procedures when suitable

Treatment aims to improve skin tone gradually while protecting the skin barrier.

Sun-Protection Guidance

  • Apply sunscreen before outdoor exposure
  • Reapply when required
  • Use protective clothing, sunglasses or an umbrella
  • Avoid prolonged midday sun exposure
  • Do not scrub tanned skin aggressively
  • Avoid bleach or unverified home remedies

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an old tan be treated?

Improvement may be possible, but treatment duration depends on the depth and cause of pigmentation.

Can one facial remove a tan permanently?

A single session may improve freshness temporarily, but lasting improvement generally requires appropriate skincare and sun protection.

Can lemon or bleach remove tanning safely?

These may irritate sensitive skin and worsen pigmentation. Dermatologist-guided treatment is safer.

Why does tanning return?

Continued UV exposure without suitable protection may cause tanning to recur.
