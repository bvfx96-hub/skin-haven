What are Causes of Hyper Pigmentation/dark
spots?
Hyperpigmentation refers to a condition where specific areas of the skin exhibit a darker
color than the surrounding skin. This occurs due to an increased production of melanin, the
pigment responsible for skin, hair, and eye coloration. Various factors contribute to
hyperpigmentation, such as sun exposure, hormonal fluctuations, genetics, specific
medications, skin inflammation or injury, and certain medical conditions. Another factor is
post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, resulting from inflammation or scarring, including
instances like acne scars.
Dark spots, also known as age spots, sun spots, or liver spots, manifest as flat, discolored
patches of skin, usually in brown or black hues. Typically appearing on sun-exposed areas
like the face, neck, hands, and arms, these spots result from an excessive production of
melanin, triggered by factors such as sun exposure, hormonal changes, certain medications,
and the natural aging process.
